## Title

Matching, Not Mandating: What Predicts Inter-Municipal Cooperation in
Ukraine's Municipalities, and Can It Be Engineered?

*(Deliberately not reusing the journal-paper working title "Collaboration
Matchmaking by Public Strategies: Evidence from a Field Experiment..." —
that title asserts the experiment as headline evidence, which doesn't exist
yet. This conference abstract leads with the retrospective finding that
does exist and frames the experiment as a preregistered design, not a
result.)*

## Abstract (~483 words)

Ukraine's hromadas — the roughly 1,400 municipal units created by the
country's 2020 territorial amalgamation — cooperate under a 2014 law
permitting voluntary inter-municipal partnerships (joint services, shared
institutions, common projects), but existing accounts of who partners with
whom rely on cross-sectional case studies that cannot separate cause from
coincidence. I reconstruct 316 dated partnership-formation events among 293 hromadas
(2015–2022) that also carry a nationally-mandated development-strategy
text, and fit a pooled formation model on the resulting dyad set, with a
contract-cluster bootstrap that corrects for a handful of large
multi-party agreements which would otherwise dominate the estimate as if
each signatory pair were an independent decision. Geographic proximity is
the only predictor with a statistically reliable effect; shared
donor-program participation is a second, more tentative signal; textual
similarity between hromadas' declared strategic goals — the flagship
signal of an NLP partner-matching pipeline I separately built for
practitioner use — does not survive as a robust predictor once multi-party
agreements are down-weighted. I read this less as evidence that shared
strategic interest is irrelevant to cooperation than as a symptom of how
partnerships have formed to date: nearly every observed agreement traces
back to hromada heads who already had a personal-contact channel, itself
strongly conditioned on physical proximity — shared district meetings,
administrative overlap, everyday visibility. Two hromadas with genuinely
complementary priorities — a shared push into local IT services, say, or
matching reconstruction-financing and EU-grant capacity needs — may never
discover each other absent that channel, leaving any thematic-fit effect
structurally unobservable in retrospective data.

This gap, not a flat null result, motivates the applied question: does
actively introducing candidate partners identified from strategy text —
closing the discovery channel that geography currently fills by default —
produce more cooperation than would occur anyway? I report the design of
a preregistered field experiment, developed in consultation with the
AIM-CC program, that randomly assigns hromada pairs to a strategy-derived
thematic-matching arm, a geographic-matching arm, and a no-facilitation
control, with reply-within-21-days as the primary outcome and a six-rung
engagement ladder (intro call through formal registry entry) as secondary
outcomes.

I discuss implications for Ukraine's decentralization-support sector,
where algorithmic partner-recommendation tools are already being piloted
on thematic-similarity logic that this retrospective data does not yet
support, and for the wider inter-municipal cooperation literature's
treatment of proximity as a background scope condition rather than a
directly tested predictor. These findings also speak to
regional-development policy more broadly: proximity-driven cooperation is
easy to explain post hoc as a spatial-economics scope condition, but this
design treats it as a directly tested mechanism rather than a background
assumption. Relevant to this conference's comparative post-Soviet
governance focus, I ask whether the same design — reading mandated
local-strategy documents, generating candidate partnerships, testing via
facilitated introduction — transfers to other decentralizing states that
require comparable local planning documents, including Kazakhstan's own
akimat-level development programs and its post-2021 shift toward elected
rural akims.

**Keywords**: inter-municipal cooperation, territorial reform, regional
development, field experiment, Ukraine
